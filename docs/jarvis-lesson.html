Part one

WHAT

What is JARVIS, and why build it?

Definition

A voice and text assistant that runs on your own Windows laptop

JARVIS is named after Tony Stark's AI in Iron Man. It is a small Node.js server plus a web page you open in Chrome or Edge. You speak or type; it understands, acts on your computer, and answers out loud.

  • Understands clear commands instantly with a rule engine, with no AI and no internet needed.
  • Thinks with an AI brain for casual, misspelled or open-ended requests: a free local model through Ollama, or a cloud model with your own API key.
  • Acts on the laptop: apps, volume, brightness, Wi-Fi, windows, files, code, dev servers, coding AIs.
  • Speaks in English, Telugu and Kannada, with two personalities: JARVIS and FRIDAY.
  • Stays safe: the server, not the AI, decides what is allowed, and "undo" reverses almost everything.

One-line summary

JARVIS = ears + brain + hands + mouth + a safety guard, all running locally.

Ears: speech recognition and a wake word.
Brain: rules first, then AI.
Hands: tools on the Node server.
Mouth: neural text-to-speech.
Guard: permission tiers, approvals, undo.

Teacher's note

Think of JARVIS as a very fast receptionist for your laptop. Simple requests ("open Chrome") it handles from memory in milliseconds. Hard questions it passes to an expert, the AI. Risky jobs it checks with you first.

Motivation

Why not just use a cloud assistant?

Problem: assistants live in the cloud

Most assistants send every word to a company's server. They cannot touch your files, your VS Code or your study plan, and they stop when the internet stops.

JARVIS: runs on your machine

The server listens only on 127.0.0.1. With Ollama, nothing leaves the laptop. Direct commands work fully offline.

Problem: students juggle many tools

A timetable app, a flashcard app, an attendance sheet, a notes app, an IDE, a PDF reader. Switching costs focus.

JARVIS: one conversation

"How many classes can I bunk?", "make flashcards from lecture3.pdf", "open dsa sprint in VS Code and ask Copilot to add tests", all in one place.

Problem: AI makes things up

A chatbot can claim it did something it never did.

JARVIS: verifies

Steps are checked after they run. A step that cannot be checked is marked ○ not checked, never a fake ✓.

Teacher's note

Good projects start from a real pain. Here the pains are privacy, scattered tools and untrustworthy AI. Every design choice later in this deck answers one of these three.

Design principles

Four rules the whole project follows

1. Rules first, AI second

A rule engine understands about 176 commands directly. It is instant, free and predictable. Only unclear or open-ended input goes to the AI. This keeps JARVIS fast even on a laptop GPU.

2. The server decides, never the AI

Every action is a named tool with a checked argument schema and a permission tier. If the AI suggests something outside the list, the server refuses it.

3. Ask before anything risky

Deleting, overwriting, changing files outside ~/jarvis, shutting down: all show an ALLOW / CANCEL card. The most dangerous actions can require Windows Hello (fingerprint, face or PIN).

4. Everything can be undone

Deleted files go to JARVIS's own trash, overwritten files keep their earlier version, and volume or to-do changes sit on an undo stack. Say "undo" and it goes back.

Why is "rules first" better for a command like "set volume to 30" than asking an AI every time?

Function · Voice & chat

Talk to it like a person

  • Tap to talk or hold the mic button; or say the wake word ("Jarvis") hands-free, even while you are in another tab.
  • It listens until you finish. Short pauses do not cut you off; it waits for a real end of sentence.
  • Three languages: English, తెలుగు and ಕನ್ನಡ, and "Tenglish" such as volume penchu (volume up).
  • Two personalities: JARVIS (formal British male voice) and FRIDAY (Indian English female voice, Neerja).
  • Context memory: "close it", "and Firefox too", "what were we talking about?" all work because a rolling summary follows the conversation.
  • Ctrl+K command palette puts every command one keystroke away; Ctrl+Shift+J works on selected text in any app.
  • Say "panic" to pause all automation instantly and "resume" to continue.

Function · Laptop control

Hands on the computer

Apps

Opens any Start-menu app by fuzzy name ("vs code", "spotify"), closes allowed ones, checks what is running.

System

Volume, brightness, dark or light theme, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, power plans, sleep, lock, shutdown (with confirmation).

Windows

Snap layouts ("put chrome left and vs code right"), show desktop, bring a window to front.

Screen

Screenshots, reading text on screen (OCR), and "look at my screen" vision with an image-capable model.

Clipboard

Read, write and search clipboard history; paste snippets; the global hotkey works on any selected text.

Files

Open and read files anywhere; write, rename, move and delete with permission outside ~/jarvis; system folders off-limits.

Teacher's note

The browser page itself cannot change your volume or open apps. It asks the Node server, and the server runs small PowerShell or Windows commands. That split between page and server is the heart of the architecture you will see in Part 3.

Attendance: the 75% bunk-o-meter

Colleges require 75% attendance. If you attended a of t classes, you can still skip floor(a / 0.75 − t) classes. If you are below, you must attend ceil((0.75t − a) / 0.25) in a row.

Example: 30 of 36 attended → you may skip 4 more.

Marks → SGPA / CGPA

Give marks and credits; JARVIS converts marks to grade points and computes SGPA = Σ(credit × grade point) / Σ credits. CGPA averages across semesters, weighted by credits.

You attended 20 of 30 classes. Are you above or below 75%, and how many must you attend in a row to reach it?

Function · Your files & notes

Ask questions about your own documents

Drop in PDFs, notes, Word, PowerPoint or Excel files and ask. JARVIS finds the right passages and answers with page numbers, so you can check the source.

  • "What do my notes say about normalization?"
  • "Summarise lecture3.pdf" or "make flashcards from lecture3.pdf"
  • "Read notes.txt", "open the agriloop folder", "does dsa sprint exist?"

This technique is called RAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation. First retrieve relevant text, then let the AI generate an answer using only that text.

Why retrieve first?

A small local model cannot read a 300-page PDF at once. Retrieval picks the 5–10 most relevant chunks, so the model reads a page, not a book. It also reduces made-up answers, because the model is told to answer from the chunks.

Function · Coding

A coding partner that can use your tools

Write, save, run

"Write a binary search in Python", then "run it". JARVIS runs .py, .js, .c and .cpp with a 10-second limit and shows output and errors.

EXPLAIN & FIX

When a program crashes, one click sends the code and the error to the AI, which explains the bug in plain words and proposes a fixed version.

DSA coach

Gives hints first and the full solution only when you ask, so you actually learn. Also a website generator and dev-server starter.

Drive other coding AIs

"open dsa sprint in vs code and ask Copilot to add tests", "ask Claude in agriloop to write unit tests". Supports VS Code Copilot, Claude Code, Gemini, Codex, Kiro, Trae, Antigravity, OpenCode, Qwen, Codebuff and Devin.

Teacher's note

For coding AIs, JARVIS does not write code itself. It opens the right tool in the right folder and pastes your request into its chat. It is a conductor, not another musician.

Function · Automation & phone

Many steps, on a schedule, from anywhere

Multi-step commands

"Open VS Code in dsa sprint, start the backend and set a 25 minute timer" becomes a checked plan of three steps.

Routines

Named bundles: "start my study mode" might mute notifications, open notes and start a focus session.

Triggers

"When I plug in my charger, set brightness to 80". A watcher on the server checks conditions with cooldowns so it does not fire repeatedly.

Phone

Reminders arrive on your phone through ntfy. Commands sent from the phone need a 6-digit authenticator code (TOTP, the same kind Google Authenticator shows). Phone commands can never approve file changes.

nlu.js · Natural Language Understanding

How rules understand messy human speech

1. Normalize

  • Remove the wake word and fillers ("hey Jarvis, please, um")
  • Fix common typos and speech errors ("main. py" → "main.py")
  • Number words to digits ("thirty" → 30)
  • Telugu / Kannada words to meaning ("penchu" → up, "tagginchu" → down)

2. Classify

  • Ordered regular-expression rules map text to an intent such as SET_VOLUME with arguments such as {level: 30}
  • Fuzzy matching by edit distance finds "vs cod" → VS Code, "atria university" vs "atria institute"
  • Guards stop statements from firing commands: "I opened chrome yesterday" is not a command

3. Context

A small memory holds the last app, file, folder and project. "Close it" resolves "it"; "and Firefox too" repeats the last action with a new target; "ask Claude to add tests" reuses the last project.

4. Confidence

Each match carries a confidence. Low confidence or no match goes to the AI planner instead of guessing. The paraphrase test checks that "confidently wrong" stays at 0.

Teacher's note

An intent is the "what" (set volume); arguments are the "details" (to 30). Almost every assistant, from Alexa to JARVIS, turns speech into intent plus arguments.

llm.js · the AI brain

One interface, many possible brains

Local: Ollama runs an open model such as qwen3.5:4b on your GPU. Free, private and offline. An RTX 3050 with 6 GB runs it comfortably; bigger models get slow.

Cloud: paste your own key for OpenAI, Anthropic (Claude), Google Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter, DeepSeek, Mistral, Together AI or any OpenAI-compatible server. JARVIS recognises the provider from the key itself: sk-ant- means Anthropic, AIza means Gemini, gsk_ means Groq.

Streaming: replies arrive word by word, so you start reading and hearing before the answer is complete. Retries handle timeouts.

Tool picking: for unclear commands the AI may choose a tool, but its choice is checked against the tool registry in agent-tools.js before anything runs.

Why keep the AI on a leash?

Language models are good at understanding and bad at being reliable. So the AI only proposes. The server disposes: it checks the tool name, the argument schema and the permission tier. A bad suggestion is refused, not run.

First reply on Ollama takes about a minute while the model loads into GPU memory, so JARVIS warms it up in the background at startup.

Keyword search

Fast, exact, needs no model. Misses synonyms.

Semantic search

Understands meaning. Needs an embedding model and more time. A file watcher re-indexes files when they change.

A scanned PDF contains pictures of pages, not text. JARVIS detects this and tells you, instead of pretending the PDF is empty.

Part four

VOICE

How JARVIS hears and speaks

voice.js · speech to text (STT)

Turning your voice into words

JARVIS uses the browser's Web Speech API (SpeechRecognition) built into Chrome and Edge. The browser streams microphone audio to its speech service and returns text.

  • Interim results arrive while you speak, so the words appear live on screen.
  • Final results arrive after each phrase.
  • Continuous mode keeps listening across pauses.
  • Language is set per request: en-IN, te-IN or kn-IN.

The utterance collector

Early versions answered after the first pause, cutting you off mid-sentence. Now a collector joins the final pieces and waits for a quiet gap before sending the whole sentence. So "open the SIH website … the smart india hackathon one" arrives as one request.

Microphone sensitivity

Speaking from far away used to show the listening animation with no words. The fix: JARVIS builds a boosted audio track with the Web Audio API (a gain node raises the volume) and hands that track to the recogniser (SpeechRecognition.start(track), Chrome 135+).

Settings offer Normal, High and Max (across the room).

wakeword.js · hands-free

"Jarvis": listening even in other tabs

Problem

Browsers pause speech recognition in background tabs. If you were on YouTube, saying "Jarvis" did nothing.

Solution: a Windows listener

The server starts a small background listener using Windows' built-in System.Speech engine. It runs offline with a tiny grammar: the word "Jarvis" plus a wildcard "anything else" grammar, so random speech is rejected instead of forced into a false match.

How the page finds out

When Windows hears "Jarvis", the server pushes a wake event over Server-Sent Events (a one-way live stream from server to page). The page comes to the front and starts full recognition.

Avoiding fights

Only one recogniser can hold the mic. A generation counter gives each start a number; old recognisers that finish late are ignored and retired, so they cannot abort the new one.

Teacher's note

Two engines, two jobs: a small offline engine that only waits for one word, and a large online engine that understands full sentences. Using a cheap detector to wake an expensive one is how phone assistants save battery too.

Path A: browser voice

The Web Speech API's speechSynthesis speaks with voices installed in Windows or the browser. Instant and offline, but can sound robotic, and Telugu or Kannada voices are often missing.

Path B: neural voice

The server opens a WebSocket to Microsoft's online neural voice service (the one Edge's "Read aloud" uses). It sends the text as SSML (Speech Synthesis Markup Language, which sets voice, rate and pitch) and receives MP3 audio chunks back. Only the reply text is sent.

Part six

SAFE

How JARVIS stays safe

Security model

Seven layers between a request and your laptop

1. Local only

The server binds to 127.0.0.1, so other computers on the network cannot reach it.

2. Same page only

Each request must come from JARVIS's own page (Origin and Sec-Fetch-Site headers), so a random website cannot call its API.

3. Tool registry

Only named tools with checked arguments exist. The AI cannot invent new ones.

4. Permission tiers

Safe actions run; risky ones preview and ask; the most dangerous can require Windows Hello.

5. Access zones

Read anywhere; ask before changing outside ~/jarvis; never touch Windows, Program Files or AppData.

6. No remote approvals

Phone commands and unattended routines can never approve a change. The card only appears at the laptop.

7. Undo and trash

Deletes go to JARVIS's trash, overwrites keep the old version, and secrets in backups are encrypted.

Panic switch

Say "panic" and all automation, routines and triggers stop until you say "resume".

Interactive · access zones

Part seven

PROOF

How do we know it works?

Testing

29 test suites, none of which touch your real data

  • Isolated server: tests start a real JARVIS whose home folder is a temporary directory, with the AI pointed at a dead port. Your files and settings are never used.
  • Page self-test: headless Edge loads the real page, controlled over the Chrome DevTools Protocol. A fake speech recogniser "speaks" commands, and laptop actions are replaced by recording fakes.
  • Route gate: the API suite fails if any server route is neither tested nor listed with a reason why it cannot be.
  • Paraphrase test: the same request said many ways. Correct, handed to the AI, or confidently wrong. The last one must stay at zero.
  • Live test (opt-in): reversible checks on the real laptop, restoring volume and brightness afterwards.

Honest limits

What JARVIS cannot do yet

Windows first

Laptop controls use Windows features. Chat, study tools and AI run elsewhere, but Windows is the supported platform.

Online speech recognition

The browser's recogniser needs internet. The wake word works offline; full sentences do not.

Small local models

A 4B model on a 6 GB GPU is limited for long reasoning. Cloud keys (Gemini, Groq free tiers) help.

Page must be open

Phone commands run inside the page, so a JARVIS tab must be open on the laptop.

Ideas for the future

  • Fully offline speech recognition (for example a Whisper model)
  • macOS and Linux controls
  • A shared knowledge base across devices
  • More languages beyond English, Telugu and Kannada
